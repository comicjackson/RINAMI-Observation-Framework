# Observed Facts

- The customer entered alone.
- The customer looked around before choosing a seat.
- A window seat was selected despite several available tables.
- The customer placed a standard lunch order.
- While waiting, the customer used a smartphone.
- The meal was completed without interruption.
- The customer remained seated for approximately ten minutes after finishing.
- Payment was completed without conversation beyond routine greetings.

No assumptions are made during this stage.

---

# Structure

Potential structural relationships identified:

```
Seat Selection
        │
        ▼
Preference for Environment

Waiting Time
        │
        ▼
Smartphone Usage

Meal Completion
        │
        ▼
Remaining Seated
```

Recurring elements:

- Preference for window seating.
- Smartphone use during waiting periods.
- Quiet, independent behavior.

---

# Hypothesis

Possible explanation:

The customer may prefer a quiet and predictable dining environment that allows uninterrupted personal time.

Prediction:

If similar environmental conditions exist during future visits, the customer is likely to:

- Choose a similar seat.
- Use a smartphone while waiting.
- Minimize unnecessary interaction.

This hypothesis is provisional and requires additional observations.

---

# Verification Plan

Future observations should examine:

- Seat selection consistency.
- Waiting behavior.
- Device usage.
- Length of stay.
- Changes under different crowd levels.

Possible outcomes:

- Supported
- Partially Supported
- Not Supported
- Revised

---

# Knowledge Record

Current confidence:

```
Low
```

Reason:

The hypothesis is based on a single observation.

Additional observations are required before drawing broader conclusions.

---

# Research Notes

This sample illustrates an important principle:

Observation records facts.

Structure identifies relationships.

Hypothesis proposes explanations.

Verification determines reliability.

Maintaining this separation helps reduce bias and improves reproducibility.
